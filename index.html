DisCo

Discrete Generation via
Continuous Flows and Diffusion

Continuous flows and diffusion for discrete generation across language, code, biology and structured scientific data: theory, methods, and open challenges.

Sydney, Australia
December 11 or 12, 2026
Call for Papers Learn More

About the Workshop

Diffusion and flow-based models have driven many of the most significant advances in generative modeling for continuous data—images, audio, and video. These methods model a noise-to-data mapping as a differential equation, enabling fast, parallel, and controllable generation. An exciting frontier is extending these methods to discrete modalities: language, code, proteins, molecules, and scientific data.

While early works such as CDCD and Diffusion-LM pioneered continuous approaches to discrete generation, concurrent breakthroughs in early 2026 showed that continuous flows can match discrete diffusion for language by flowing Gaussian noise to one-hot embeddings. This has sparked rapid follow-up research and a surge of interest in the community.

DisCo brings together the researchers driving this progress to consolidate the taxonomy of existing methods, accelerate the development of new approaches, identify shared benchmarks and open problems, and support the growth of a more coherent research community.

Key questions
  • What theoretical foundations, training paradigms, and evaluation standards are needed to ensure consistent progress across discrete modalities?
  • What are the empirical trade-offs between continuous and discrete generative processes, and what is required for continuous flows to match the performance of discrete methods at scale?
  • How can the smooth, differentiable structure of continuous processes be used to enable controllable generation, conditioning, and alignment?

Topics of Interest

We invite submissions spanning theory, methods, applications, critical analyses, open questions, and negative results.

Flows and diffusion across discrete modalities
  • Language, code, molecules, proteins, biological sequences, graphs, and other scientific or structured discrete data.
  • Modality-specific properties and challenges for continuous generative processes.
  • Controllable generation through gradient-based guidance and reward alignment.
Method design and scaling
  • Training objectives, noise schedules, and embeddings (e.g., learned, geometric).
  • Distillation for acceleration; consistency models and flow map matching for few-step generation.
  • Scaling laws, data efficiency, and compute-optimal training.
Comparisons with existing approaches
  • Continuous vs. discrete diffusion trade-offs; quality–speed trade-off in few-step regimes.
  • Understanding the gap with autoregression; hybrid methods.
  • Negative results and limitations of current methods.
Evaluation and empirical understanding
  • Standardized benchmarks and fair evaluation at academic scales.
  • Metrics beyond generative perplexity.
  • Self-conditioning and other empirically important techniques that may outpace theory.
  • Connections to phenomena in image-domain diffusion.

Call for Papers

We welcome original unpublished work and work under review. Published work at NeurIPS 2026 or other venues will not be accepted.

Paper track
  • Extended abstracts or 4–8 page papers (excluding references and appendices).
  • Non-archival. Double-blind review by 3 reviewers on OpenReview.
  • Accepted papers designated as oral or poster. Best paper award for the top contribution.
Blogpost track
  • Max 2,000 words on key ideas or papers in the field.
  • Accessible format for sharing insights and opinions. Non-archival.
  • See the blogpost track page for formatting guidelines and details.

Per NeurIPS 2026 policy, authors must disclose LLM use. Submission link will be available soon.
